How much do Facebook and Instagram ads cost in NZ?

By Ephraim Stocks, founder of Melu Media · 26 October 2026

Short answer: you choose the budget. Meta has no fixed price per ad. You set a daily or total budget and pay for the views or clicks the auction gives you.

Skip the Boost button. Set ads up in Ads Manager with a clear goal, such as messages or bookings, and judge them on what each enquiry costs.

Facebook and Instagram ads are bought through one tool, Meta Ads Manager. That's why the two platforms are priced together. This guide explains how the charging works, why boosting posts is usually money wasted, and how to pick a first budget for a business that sells to people in Christchurch.

How Meta charges

Every time there is space for an ad in someone's feed, stories or reels, Meta runs an auction between the advertisers trying to reach that person. You don't name a price per ad. You set:

  • A budget, either daily or a total for the campaign.
  • A goal, such as reach, website visits, messages or leads.
  • An audience, such as people within a set distance of your shop.

Meta then spends the budget trying to get the most of that result. A daily budget is an average, not a hard cap. Ads Manager tells you how far: in some accounts Meta says it may spend up to 75% over the daily budget on days with better opportunities, and in others up to 25%. Either way, the weekly total is held to about seven times the daily budget. So a $20 a day budget might spend $35 on one day, and still stays around $140 for the week.

Why a small audience fills up

For a business that only serves part of Christchurch, the audience is small. Once most of those people have seen your ad several times, spending more buys repeat views of the same people rather than new ones. That's the point to change the ad, not raise the budget.

What makes ads cost more

  • A small or very narrow audience. Fewer people means more competition for each view.
  • Busy seasons. More advertisers bid in the run-up to Christmas and big sale weekends, so the same budget buys less.
  • The goal you pick. A lead or a purchase costs more than a view, because fewer people do it.
  • Weak creative. Ads people scroll past get shown less efficiently. A reel filmed for the platform usually does better than a resized poster.

Why you shouldn't boost posts

The Boost button is the easiest way to spend money on Facebook and Instagram, and usually the least effective. Boosting pays to show a post that was made for your followers to more people. It wasn't made to win new customers, and the setup gives you very little control over what you are paying for.

  • You pay for attention, not customers. Many boosts end up chasing likes, comments and views. They look good on the post and rarely turn into calls, bookings or sales.
  • One post, one version. You can't test two openings or two offers against each other, so you never learn what works.
  • Fewer controls. Ads Manager lets you choose the exact goal, where the ad appears and how the budget is split. Boosting offers a short list of options.
  • Hard to judge. Results come back as reach and reactions, not what each enquiry cost you.

Set the campaign up in Ads Manager instead: pick the result you want, such as messages or bookings, run a few versions of the ad, and keep the ones that bring enquiries at a cost that makes sense for your business.

Setting a first budget

  1. Pick one goal. Messages, calls, bookings or website visits. Not all four.
  2. Set the area. Draw the radius around where your customers actually come from.
  3. Choose a budget you can hold for four weeks. A test that stops after five days tells you very little.
  4. Run two or three versions of the ad. Different first seconds of video, or different offers.
  5. Check results weekly. Look at cost per result, not just reach or likes.

Agency fees are separate

If someone manages the ads for you, their fee is on top of the budget you pay Meta. Ask whether the fee covers making the ad creative, or only setting up and running the campaign.

Common questions

Do Facebook and Instagram ads cost the same?

They run through the same auction in Ads Manager. Meta can place one campaign on both, and costs differ by placement.

What is the smallest budget I can use?

Meta's minimum depends on your currency and campaign goal. Ads Manager shows a warning if the budget you type is too low.

How long before ads work?

Give a campaign at least two to four weeks. Meta's system needs time to learn who responds, and you need enough results to compare.
