How often should a small business post on Instagram?

By Ephraim Stocks, founder of Melu Media · 26 October 2026

Short answer: there's no official number. For most local businesses, two or three feed posts or reels a week, plus stories on most days, is a good place to start.

Keeping it up for months matters more than posting a lot for two weeks.

Instagram has never published a required posting rate. Its head, Adam Mosseri, has said in his own videos that there's no single answer: posting more tends to reach more people, but not if it costs you quality or burns you out. Marketing guides land in a similar place. Shopify suggests at least three to five feed posts a week and a story at least once a day.

That's a fair target for a brand with a marketing team. For a café, shop or trade business where the owner is also doing everything else, it helps to start smaller and build up.

A schedule a busy business can keep

FormatStarting pointWhat it's for
Reels1 to 2 a weekReaching people who don't follow you yet
Photo or carousel posts1 a weekShowing products, prices, finished work
StoriesMost daysKeeping followers up to date: today's specials, new stock, behind the scenes

This is our recommendation for local businesses, not a rule from Instagram. Raise it once the habit is in place and you can see which posts bring results.

Why reels matter most for reach

Instagram says most of what people see in Reels comes from accounts they don't follow. For a local business, that's how new customers in your area find you. Photo posts and stories mostly reach people who already follow you, which is still useful for repeat customers.

Facebook can show the same reels. Sharing each reel to your Facebook page as well costs nothing extra and reaches a different, often older, audience.

How to keep it going

Film in one go

The schedules that last are the ones filmed in batches. A couple of hours of filming once a month can cover four to eight reels and a set of photos, which fills most of the month. Stories are the exception: they work best filmed on the day.

Plan the month on one page

List what is happening at the business that month: new products, events, seasonal changes, public holidays. Each one is a post. Our list of what to film is usually the same few things: the people, the product being made or used, the finished result, and a customer's view of walking in.

Schedule ahead

Meta Business Suite lets you schedule posts and reels to Instagram and Facebook in advance, for free. Schedule the week on Monday and you're not thinking about it again until Friday.

An example month for a café

  • Week 1: a reel of the barista making the new seasonal drink, and a photo post of the menu with prices.
  • Week 2: a reel of a dish from the kitchen to the table, and a reel introducing a staff member.
  • Week 3: a reel of the morning set-up before opening, and a carousel of the cabinet food.
  • Week 4: a reel promoting the weekend special, and a photo post of the space.
  • Every day: a story or two of today's specials, deliveries, or customers (with their OK).

That's eight feed posts from one shoot, plus stories. We film this kind of content for restaurants and cafés, and the same plan works for shops and trades with different subjects.

Is posting more always better?

No. Posting daily for three weeks and then going quiet for two months looks worse than posting twice a week every week. Customers check your page before they visit. An account that last posted in March makes people wonder whether you're still open.

How to tell if it's working

Instagram's built-in insights show what each post did. The useful numbers for a local business are:

  • Accounts reached, especially non-followers, which shows whether reels are finding new people.
  • Profile visits and link taps, which show interest beyond a like.
  • Messages and calls, which are closest to real enquiries.

Ask new customers how they found you. It's the most reliable measure a small business has.

Common questions

Is it bad to post every day?

No, if each post is worth watching. It only becomes a problem when quality drops or you can't keep it up.

What is the best time to post?

Check the "Most active times" view in your Instagram insights. It is based on your own followers, which beats any general rule.

Should I post the same thing on Facebook?

Usually yes. Sharing reels and posts to both is little extra work and reaches people who only use one of them.
